## Week 2 – Aerodynamics

### Key Concepts
- Aerodynamics studies airflows and their effect on aircraft performance.
- Only two physical surface forces exist: **pressure** (normal to surface) and **shear stress** (tangential).
- Fluid (air) continuously deforms under shear.
- Four main forces on an aircraft in flight:
  - Lift \( L \)
  - Drag \( D \)
  - Weight \( W \) (force = mass × g)
  - Thrust \( T \)
- Straight-and-level flight equilibrium: \( L = W \), \( T = D \)

### Core Formulas

**Lift equation**
\[
L = C_L \times S \times \left( \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 \right) = C_L \, q \, S
\]

**Drag equation**
\[
D = C_D \times S \times \left( \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 \right) = C_D \, q \, S
\]

**Dynamic pressure**
\[
q = \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2
\]

**Ideal gas law**
\[
P = \rho R T \qquad R = 287\,\text{J·kg}^{-1}\text{·K}^{-1}\text{ (air)}
\]

**Temperature conversion**
\[
T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273
\]

**Continuity equation (mass flow rate constant)**
\[
\dot{m} = \rho_1 A_1 V_1 = \rho_2 A_2 V_2
\]
For incompressible flow (\( \rho = \) constant):
\[
A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2
\]

**Bernoulli’s equation (incompressible, inviscid, steady flow)**
\[
P_T = P + \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 = \text{constant}
\]
or
\[
P_1 + \frac{1}{2} \rho V_1^2 = P_2 + \frac{1}{2} \rho V_2^2
\]

**Pitot-static tube (true airspeed)**
\[
V = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho} (P_{\text{Total}} - P_{\text{Static}})}
\]

**Equivalent airspeed** (calibrated with sea-level density)
\[
V_e = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho_{sl}} (P_{\text{Total}} - P_{\text{Static}})}
\]

### Important Variables
| Symbol | Name | Description | Units |
|--------|------|-------------|-------|
| \( L \) | Lift | Force perpendicular to the free-stream direction generated by pressure difference | N |
| \( D \) | Drag | Force parallel to the free-stream direction opposing motion | N |
| \( C_L \) | Lift coefficient | Dimensionless measure of lift efficiency | – |
| \( C_D \) | Drag coefficient | Dimensionless measure of drag | – |
| \( S \) | Wing reference area | Projected wing area (usually includes fuselage carry-through) | m² |
| \( q \) | Dynamic pressure | Kinetic energy per unit volume of the free stream | Pa |
| \( \rho \) | Air density | Mass per unit volume of air | kg/m³ |
| \( V \) | True airspeed | Actual speed of aircraft relative to air | m/s |
| \( P \) | Static pressure | Thermodynamic pressure of the air | Pa |
| \( R \) | Specific gas constant | For air ≈ 287 J/(kg·K) | J/(kg·K) |

## Week 3 – Airfoils

### Key Concepts
- An airfoil is a cross-sectional shape designed to produce lift.
- Higher pressure on the lower surface than upper surface produces net upward force.
- Streamlines show the path of fluid particles in steady flow.
- Angle of attack \( \alpha \) is the angle between the chord line and the free-stream velocity.

### Airfoil Terminology
- **Leading edge (LE)** – front of the airfoil
- **Trailing edge (TE)** – rear of the airfoil
- **Chord (\( c \))** – straight line from LE to TE
- **Camber** – curvature of the mean line
- **Thickness** – usually expressed as fraction of chord (e.g. 0.12\( c \) = 12 % chord)
- **Aerodynamic centre (AC)** – point about which pitching moment is constant (≈ quarter-chord for low-speed airfoils)

### Core Formulas

**Section lift coefficient (2-D)**
\[
c_l = a_0 \alpha + c_{l,\alpha=0}
\]
or
\[
c_l = a_0 (\alpha - \alpha_{L=0})
\]

**Ideal thin-airfoil lift slope**
\[
a_0 = 2\pi \quad \text{(per radian)}
\]

**Moment coefficient**
\[
M = C_M \left( \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 \right) S c
\]

**Reynolds number**
\[
Re = \frac{\rho_\infty V_\infty L}{\mu_\infty} = \frac{\text{inertial forces}}{\text{viscous forces}}
\]

### Lift Curve & Stall
- Linear region: \( c_l \) increases with \( \alpha \)
- Stall occurs when flow separates from the upper surface → sudden loss of lift (\( c_{l,\max} \) reached)
- Cambered airfoils produce positive lift at zero angle of attack

## Week 4 – Wings (Finite Wings)

### Key Concepts
- Finite wings produce **wing-tip vortices** because high pressure on the lower surface flows around the tips to the low-pressure upper surface.
- Tip vortices induce **downwash** behind the wing, reducing the effective angle of attack.
- This leads to **induced drag**.

### Core Formulas

**Aspect ratio**
\[
AR = \frac{b^2}{S} = \frac{b}{\bar{c}}
\]

**Wing area**
\[
S = b \times \bar{c}
\]
(\( \bar{c} \) = average chord)

**Finite-wing lift curve slope** (for \( AR \ge 4 \))
\[
a = \frac{a_0}{1 + \frac{a_0}{\pi AR e}}
\]
(\( a_0 \) in rad)

**Drag polar**
\[
C_D = C_{D,0} + \frac{C_L^2}{\pi AR e}
\]

**Induced drag coefficient**
\[
C_{D,i} = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi AR e}
\]

**Parasite drag** \( C_{D,0} \) consists of:
- Skin-friction drag (shear)
- Pressure (form) drag
- Interference drag

### Span Efficiency Factor \( e \)
- \( e = 1 \) for ideal elliptical lift distribution
- Typically 0.7–0.9 for real wings

## Week 5 – Materials

### Key Concepts
- **Stress** is force per unit area.
- **Normal stress** \( \sigma \) acts perpendicular to a surface (tension/compression).
- **Shear stress** \( \tau \) acts parallel to a surface.
- Strength is the maximum stress a material can withstand in a particular failure mode before permanent deformation or fracture.

### Core Formulas

**Normal stress**
\[
\sigma = \frac{F}{A}
\]

**Shear stress**
\[
\tau = \frac{F}{A}
\]

**Bending stress**
\[
\sigma = \frac{M y}{I}
\]

**Second moment of area (rectangle)**
\[
I = \frac{b h^3}{12}
\]

**Factor of Safety**
\[
FS = \frac{F_{\text{fail}}}{F_{\text{allow}}} = \frac{\sigma_{\text{fail}}}{\sigma_{\text{allow}}}
\]

**Rule of Mixtures (unidirectional composite)**
\[
E_1 = E_f V_f + E_m V_m, \qquad V_f + V_m = 1
\]

## Week 6 – Structures

### Key Concepts
- Aircraft structures are mostly **semi-monocoque**: thin skins stabilised by stiffeners (stringers, frames, ribs, spars).
- Main functions of an aircraft structure:
  1. Carry flight loads
  2. Maintain aerodynamic shape
  3. Protect occupants/payload
  4. Provide attachment points for systems

### Buckling
Critical failure mode for thin and slender members under compression.

**Euler column buckling**
\[
P_{CR} = \frac{\pi^2 E I}{(k L)^2}
\]

**Plate buckling**
\[
\sigma_{CR} = K E \left( \frac{t}{b} \right)^2
\]

- Longer columns and wider plates buckle more easily.
- Increasing \( I \) (by moving material farther from the neutral axis) raises buckling load dramatically (reason for I-beams and stiffened skins).

### Thin Plates
- Excellent in tension and shear.
- Poor in compression and pure bending unless stiffened.

## Quick Reference – Most Important Relationships

- Higher \( AR \) → lower induced drag
- Higher \( C_L \) → higher induced drag (quadratic)
- Stall occurs at \( C_{L,\max} \)
- Buckling is a geometric instability, not a pure material failure
- Density decreases with altitude → true airspeed > equivalent airspeed for the same indicated value
- Bernoulli links pressure and velocity: faster flow → lower static pressure (source of lift)
